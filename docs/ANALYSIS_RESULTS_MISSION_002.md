# GulfScope annual trend results

The Mission 001 monthly NASA POWER snapshot yielded **540 complete months and 45 complete calendar years (1981–2025)**. Each monthly input variable has 540 finite values and no fill values. Repeated builds generated byte-identical CSVs and manifests. SHA-256 checks confirmed both raw files stayed intact during the build and testing.

Mean air temperature showed a statistically significant increasing monotonic trend in the sampled MERRA-2 grid series at familywise α = 0.05. No statistically significant monotonic trend in estimated precipitation accumulation was detected under the specified test and threshold. Precipitation changes of either sign remain compatible with the primary interval. The minimum-temperature test is unavailable because the original snapshot contains monthly minima. The day-weighted average of those minima is retained as a descriptive field.

The lag screen used ±0.2922. Block-length sensitivity at three, six, and ten years gave temperature intervals of [0.3145, 0.5311], [0.3173, 0.5323], and [0.3222, 0.5277] °C per decade. The respective precipitation intervals were [−13.469, 0.435], [−12.855, −0.038], and [−12.442, −0.905] mm per decade. Precipitation's upper endpoint changes sign across lengths, so its marginal interval is sensitive to the resampling assumptions. The Holm-adjusted Hamed–Rao decision remains the primary significance assessment.

MERRA-2 combines a model and assimilated observations on a 0.5° latitude × 0.625° longitude grid. These trends characterize the reanalysis grid estimate sampled at the Bahrain coordinates. They cannot establish station-level changes, resolve individual reclaimed areas, or attribute changes to reclamation.
